Animals — herds & flocks

Every group of animals you run. 1) + Add group for each herd or flock, and put it on a paddock. 2) Move records a move; 💰 Sale plan figures what a group is worth when it sells. 3) Breeding clocks and youngstock batches live under Breeding below.

Animal Units (AU)

1 AU = 1,000 lb of live weight. AU drives the stocking-density and forage math in this app. A 1,100 lb cow ≈ 1.1 AU; a 120 lb goat ≈ 0.12 AU; a 4 lb hen ≈ 0.004 AU. Daily forage demand is figured at ~26 lb of dry matter per AU per day — a planning estimate, not a ration.

Paddocks & grazing

Every field, by its name or number. Each card shows whether the paddock is being grazed, resting, or ready, the last forage height, and any condition notes. Log a forage reading or a before/after photo from the card. New paddocks are drawn on the Map page.

Records

Everything you've written down, by kind. Pick a tab — moves, rainfall, feed, water, trips, breeding. Field notes holds observations (flooded, fence down, a sick animal) plus anything Tell FarmerHelp couldn't place — kept in your exact words so nothing gets lost.

Breeding, breeds & youngstock

Breeding clocks and sale dates. 1) Set eggs or Record a breeding — FarmerHelp counts down to hatch or due day from the breed's own figures. 2) Log the outcome and the youngstock batch makes itself, with its sale-ready date. 3) The Sell-by planner works backwards from a sale date (Thanksgiving, Easter…) to when you must start.

Farm planning

Water lines and buildings, planned before you spend. Place water sources, draw buried or surface lines, and get lengths, fittings, and a cost estimate. Structures (hoop coops, chicken tractors) get a materials list figured from their size. All prices are yours to edit — these are planning estimates, not quotes.

Farm money

The money story of the farm. Money in & out is the simple ledger. Revenue & sale plans shows what each group or batch should make (and what it actually made). Projections looks 3–5 years ahead from your own records — always labelled an estimate.

Reports

Your farm in numbers, for a season or a year. Every figure is figured from your own records — nothing is made up. Pick a range, read the sections, then Download CSV for a spreadsheet or Print for paper.

Every number below is computed from your own records — moves, feed logs, rain gauge, water checks, and the money ledger. Nothing is invented.

Gardens

Garden plots and what you planted in them. 1) Add a plot with its size. 2) Log each planting — FarmerHelp figures the harvest window from the crop's own days and your frost dates in Settings. 3) When you pick, log the harvest. Garden plots are never grazed — the grazing advice ignores them.

About this app

FarmerHelp v2.2 — satellite mapping with NAIP farm imagery, multiple properties, breeding & breed library, feed / hay / water tracking, photos, water & structure planning, trip costs, farm finances, sale plans & revenue, farm reports, a forage planting planner, a fertilizer & amendment log, a garden planner, a unified Reminders page, and Tell FarmerHelp voice commands (on-device). Everything is stored on this device. Tip: long-press the FarmerHelp icon on your home screen → Tell FarmerHelp opens voice capture directly.

Settings

Set the farm up your way. Properties — add, rename, or reposition each piece of land (this is the one place coordinates are edited). Weather normally follows the property on its own. Vehicles, your data backup, and the legal terms live here too.

Farm setup

Weather comes from Open-Meteo (free, no account). It only loads when you're online and tap update — the rest of the app works fully offline.

Locations (properties)

Run more than one place? Add each property here. The active property drives the map, the weather, water checks, and Today's notices. Herds, breeding, and money stay farm-wide.

Weather

The forecast loads automatically for the active property — on open, and whenever you switch properties. Normally that's all you need. The controls below are for the exceptions: pin weather to one property no matter which is active, or nudge the exact point the forecast is pulled for (say your rain gauge sits in the valley but the property pin is up at the house).

Grazing engine settings

Rest days needed before a paddock is "ready" again, by season. Defaults are common rotational-grazing starting points — tune them to your land and forage.

Forage library & planting

The planting planner's seed library — windows, rates, and prices are yours to tune. Planting windows follow your frost dates below.

Defaults (Apr 10 / Nov 1) are southern Great Plains estimates — set yours from your own records or your county extension.

Your data

Everything lives in this browser on this device — nothing is uploaded. Back up regularly; an export is the only way to move your farm to a new device.

Vehicles (for trip costs)

Up to 3 vehicles. Fuel cost per trip is figured from miles ÷ MPG × price per gallon.

Water & hay defaults

Buried lines below the frost line in your area stay ice-free longest — 18 in is a mild-climate starting point; colder areas often go 24–36 in. Check local frost depth before you dig.
